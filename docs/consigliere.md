# The Consigliere and the board

The Consigliere hands out the work in a room. The board is the room's record of every request and answer, and `rooms-board` is the command that reads and writes it.

Instead of handing out the work yourself, you can talk to one member and let him do it. "Research this with two others, discuss your differences, and bring me the result": he brings the others in, gives them work, reads their answers, runs the discussion and reports back. That member is the room's **Consigliere**. Any member can be one; Backroom ships no preset and names no model.

The board does the carrying and Backroom keeps the record. The Consigliere gives work by posting on the board (`rooms-board post "@name the job"`): each post is a numbered request on that member's thread, and the member's final reply answers it and wakes him as a new turn, with the member's T3 status at the top. He never waits: he posts, ends his turn with his note to you, and is woken by every reply of a member. Members never post to each other, so he routes reviews: he sends the worker's result to the reviewer by post number, and the reviewer's findings back to the worker, until the reviewer passes it. To adjust work in flight he corrects the request he posted (`rooms-board correct <number> "…"`), and to replace it he cancels it and posts the new job. Backroom marks him, tells everyone the rules, records every post and answer, and shows it. Every judgement is his: who works on what, whether another round is needed, whether the members agree, when to stop. Backroom counts no votes and sets no limits; you see the spend grow and can stop anything.

- **Choose one for new rooms.** **Settings → Consigliere…** lists your saved crew, with **None**. Once one is chosen, every new room opens with him seated as its Consigliere: you talk to him there, and he brings in the others. With **None**, a new room opens empty and you seat members from **Members**. Deleting that saved crew member asks first and clears the setting; members already seated stay.
- **Or make one in a room you have.** Under **Members**, a member's menu has **Make Consigliere** and, on the one who is, **Remove as Consigliere**. You are told first, and may go on, when he is in plan mode or his permission mode asks for approval (he would be stopped at every board command), or when he runs on a harness other than Claude, Codex or Antigravity (untried there; the board command is a shell command, so any harness that runs commands should do). A room has at most one; removing him from the room clears the mark.
- **Two views.** A room with a Consigliere opens on your **chat** with him (your messages to him, everything he says that is not addressed to a member, and the notices that need you: his own failed turn on your message shows there with **Retry** and **Dismiss**, the same notice as in the status bar); the **Board · N** tab beside his name shows every post in the room. The chat is a view of the board; nothing is kept for it alone. His posts go there by whom they are addressed to, not by their kind: one that wakes a member, answers a member's request or is about one (his requests, corrections, cancels and retries to members, his answers to them, a late answer or a cancel of a closed request that wakes nobody too, and a post to you and a member at once) is on the board only; the rest of what he says is in the chat (his answers to you and to Backroom, his updates, follow-ups and notes, his one-line replies to keep-alives, his retries of his own failed turns). The composer is under both. Posts show their number (`#51`), because his reports cite them; a post's number is the same on the page, in every briefing and in `rooms-board`. The board's own records (a run, for one) take numbers too, so the posts' numbers can skip.
- **Every message is for him.** Whatever you type in such a room goes to the Consigliere, also when it names members: `@bob check the logs` tells him who you want on it, and he sends the work. Like every message, it goes into his turn when he is working, and is a turn of its own when he is idle, also when it addresses him by name (`@fable deploy it`). His first reply to your message answers it and notifies you; his later replies are updates in the room's news, brief ones included. A T3 slash command (`@bob /compact`) still goes straight to the member it names, with no relay; the board carries no slash commands to members.
- **Members, not subagents.** The people he brings in are seated members, each on its own T3 thread, so each keeps what it has read and said through every round. He seats them from your saved crew; in a room with a Consigliere only he may. Any member may still use subagents inside its own task.
- **His rules are yours to change.** **Settings → Consigliere…** shows the rules as text; edit them to your liking and **Save rules**, or **Reset to default**. They apply to every Consigliere, in every room, from his next message. How the board works (every reply wakes him, nothing is held, he routes reviews, keep-alives, messages into running turns) is added after them whatever their wording.
- **What his retiring does to a thread.** In the same dialog, **When he retires a member** says what happens to a member's T3 thread when he retires it with `rooms-board retire`, or when a reseat replaces it with a fresh seat: **Keep in T3** (the default), **Settle**, **Archive** or **Delete in T3**, the same choices as removing a member yourself (Delete asks first). A thread also seated in another room is always kept, and his reply from `retire` says what was done.
- **What he is told.** His first briefing carries his rules, and so does the one after his context was compacted or you changed them. The default rules are `ENGINE_CONSIGLIERE_RULES` in [`src/app/consigliere.ts`](../src/app/consigliere.ts), the text **Settings → Consigliere…** shows until you save your own: how he scopes and delegates work, how many members he keeps seated, how reviews and reseats go, and when he ends his turn.
- **What the others are told.** His messages arrive on a member's thread bare, so each other member gets a **seat briefing** once, as its first turn: who the Consigliere is, that his requests are work on your behalf and other members' messages are statements, that its final reply is its answer (with its T3 status at the top) and there is nothing else to run, that it does not post to other members or ask for reviews itself, how to read the board, that `rooms-board rules` and `rooms-board state` print these facts and the room state again, and that it may use subagents in its own task. It asks for a one-line acknowledgement. It is sent when a member is seated in such a room and to everyone already seated when a room gets its first Consigliere; when the mark moves, everyone is told who he is now. Both are Backroom's requests, not yours (the board shows "Backroom → @sol: seat briefing"): the acknowledgement goes to nobody, and when a member cannot run its briefing (a harness that is not signed in, a usage limit), the failure is the Consigliere's notice and never on your **Needs you**. He can `rooms-board cancel` the briefing, and `rooms-board retire` takes a briefing the member never answered with it. A reseat's checkpoint and its call-off are Backroom's requests in the same way (see below).
- **The standing part goes out once.** A thread's first briefing carries who it is, the rules, where everyone works, the browsers, the board command and the room state, and the Consigliere's carries **Your crew** too: your saved crew, one line each with the name, the model, the effort level, a note when it works in a worktree of its own, and what it is good at and should avoid (each when you wrote it), the lines `rooms-board crew` prints. Later briefings carry the work (its requests and corrections, the answers to what it asked, the posts it has not seen) under one line naming `rooms-board rules`, which prints the standing part again. A part that changes goes out again by itself, under "Changed since your last briefing": your edit of his rules or of your crew (what one is good at or should avoid, a model, an effort level, where one works, someone added or deleted; not a look or a permission mode), a member's own folder or branch (or someone now sharing it), the room's browsers turned on or off. Seats coming and going and a browser starting or stopping do not count. After a compaction, whether `@name /compact` or one Claude Code made itself (T3 records both on the thread), the next briefing carries all of it again.
- **The room state.** The Consigliere keeps a summary of where the room stands with `rooms-board state "<text>"`: your standing decisions, the plan and where each item stands, who holds what, open questions. Each write replaces the last (at most 8,000 characters), anyone in the room prints it with `rooms-board state`, and every new seat's first briefing carries it, a Consigliere who takes over among them, so a fresh seat starts from it rather than from an old transcript. You read it at the top of the room, under its name (closed to its first line until you open it, marked new when it changed since you last did), and its first line is the room's line on its Overview tile; a new state shows there without a reload.

## Members only answer

The board is the room's journal (`data/board.sqlite`), and the members only answer.

- **A member's final reply is its answer** to the requests its turn was given, and it wakes the Consigliere. The post starts with the member's T3 status: idle, or what still runs (a turn, background tasks, subagents, by name). Nothing holds a request open: neither background work nor a review keeps it open.
- **Later turns are follow-ups.** A turn the member starts itself (when a background task it left running ends), one typed in T3 Code, or one another agent's message started is posted as a follow-up on the member's request, with its status, and wakes the Consigliere. Background work that ends without a further turn gets one follow-up from Backroom after 30 s of quiet, so he learns that it ended.
- **Members do not post.** `rooms-board post` is the Consigliere's (you post from the page). Members keep `read`, `index`, `members`, `rules`, `state`, `keepalive` (to read the setting) and `crew`. In a room without a Consigliere, any member may also seat, write the room state and set keep-alives. Members never post to each other, so the Consigliere routes reviews.
- **Corrections and cancels.** A correction goes into the member's running turn like any message (or its next turn, when the running one takes nothing: see [Sending while someone is working](messages.md#sending-while-someone-is-working)), and that turn's reply answers the request with it; only one that comes in as the turn ends, too late to go in, leaves that reply answering nothing, and the next turn brings it and answers. A cancel applies at once: it stops the member's turn when that turn has nothing else to work on, and otherwise goes into the running turn like any message (the member is told to stop work on it, and its reply answers the rest), so no turn of its own follows.
- **A failed turn** reaches the Consigliere and whoever asked for the request as an error notice on the board; when you asked, you see it under **Needs you**. **Retry** (or `rooms-board retry <number>`) runs it again with what it was given. A turn woken by a cancel alone (a member whose thread keeps failing, woken by the cancel of the request it failed) is on the board as a "Recovery failure" and needs nobody: there is nothing to retry, and the failure that matters is the one on the request. A turn woken by a notice is another matter: when the Consigliere, told of a member's failure, fails too, the room has stalled, and that "Recovery failure" is yours, with **Retry**. A turn that fails again after a retry says which failure on the request it is ("This is the second failure on this request, after a retry."), so he reports it instead of retrying once more.
- **A member held where no reply ends it** is one notice to the Consigliere (in a room with one) on each open request of the member's: an approval, a secret or a question only you can answer (naming what is asked, and the subagent when one of its subagents asks), a usage limit (until when, and whether T3 continues it), a queue T3 holds, or its thread settled, archived or deleted in T3 while seated. It goes once per request per hold, as T3 tells you once per run per state: a hold is the state and what holds it (the approval, secret or question T3 waits on, the limited run, the held queue's run), so the same hold through ticks and restarts posts nothing more, a new one on the same request (a second approval after a retry, another limited run) posts again, and one that clears posts nothing (the member's next reply is the news), and working, a tool running, a task list changing or a check moving is never posted as a hold (a turn gone silent is the next bullet's). It sits on the request it holds and shows under it; a request answered or cancelled, or a member retired, gets no more. A paused turn's own notice already says it is paused, on the requests that turn was given, so a usage-limit notice goes only to requests no notice of that same run covers; the Consigliere's own thread gets none, since what holds it is yours and on your page.
- **A member silent in a running turn** is one notice to the Consigliere on each open request of the member's when T3 has delivered no new item on its running turn for five minutes, and one more at each doubling while the silence lasts (10, 20, 40 minutes and on); a new item ends the spell. The silence is measured from the last item's time as T3 records it, so a restart of the service neither delays nor repeats a notice. It names the member, the minutes and the last item (its type, a command's input, a tool's name or a message's first line, its status and when it started), and says that a long command looks the same until it returns. Backroom stops, restarts and corrects nothing: the Consigliere checks or waits, and reports to you if it does not end. A member held in one of the states above, a member with no running turn (idle, or only background tasks or a pull-request watch), a member with no open request, and the Consigliere himself are not told silent.
- **A watched run that finished with a failed check** is one notice to the Consigliere: a pull request watched on any seat's thread, his own included, that goes from a check pending to none pending with one failed (see [Background status](room.md#background-status)). It sits on no request and goes once per run: a rerun on the same head is a run of its own and is told again, the same finished run posts nothing more through ticks and restarts, and a run the service first sees already finished (started while it was down) posts nothing; none goes for a run whose checks all pass or while a check is still pending.
- **Keep-alives.** A thread's prompt cache lasts an hour, and rereading a large thread cold costs many times a warm wake (for a 700k context, about 9 dollars on Fable and 3.50 on Opus, against about 25 cents warm). So while the Consigliere waits on work in flight (a member still working, or background work or a subagent of his own) and nothing has woken him for 55 minutes, Backroom wakes him with a keep-alive post: a line saying there is nothing to do, then the status lines of that work. He replies in one line and uses no tools, and the reply is a note that wakes nobody; it shows in your chat with him, like everything he says that is addressed to no member. A keep-alive never goes out with nothing in flight or while his own turn runs, and a turn is followed by at most one keep-alive per interval: the next comes only an interval after the keep-alive's own turn. Members get keep-alives only when the Consigliere turns them on for them. `rooms-board keepalive` shows the setting; the Consigliere changes it with `keepalive @name on|off` and `keepalive every <minutes>` (1 to 59, the room's own interval; 55 or less leaves the wake time to reach the model within the hour), and `ROOMS_KEEPALIVE_MS` sets the interval for rooms that set none. Work that never ends (a dev server left running) keeps a seat warm without end, one keep-alive per interval (about 6.50 dollars a day for a large Consigliere context): have it stopped, or turn keep-alives off for that seat; there is no cap.

## The board, and `bin/rooms-board`

The board is the room's saved record: every request and answer, who sent it, to whom, and how the turn ended. It holds no conclusions. Backroom never records that members agree or that an answer is right; the Consigliere says such things in his own words, citing posts, and you can open them and check him.

- **Everything is a numbered post.** Requests, answers, corrections, cancels, notes, follow-ups and notices (a failed turn, a retry) are posts, each with its sender and receiver; an answer says which request it answers. A message a member sends another with T3's own thread tools is recorded as a note by its sender, naming the receiver; it makes no request.
- **Members read the board with one command.** T3's tools read one thread at a time; `bin/rooms-board` reaches the room. Every briefing says how to call it, with the member's own key (the one `rooms-browser` uses), and every command works within that member's room only:

  ```sh
  bin/rooms-board index --as sol.a3a51677              # the posts: number, kind, sender → receiver, state, size; no text
  bin/rooms-board index --after 51 --as sol.a3a51677   # only later posts; --all adds the board's own records (runs, subscriptions)
  bin/rooms-board read 51 53 --as sol.a3a51677         # the exact saved text; over 20,000 characters comes in pages (--page 2)
  bin/rooms-board members --as sol.a3a51677            # who is seated: model, what each is doing on the board and in T3 (as the page's label says it), its requests, context, thread
  bin/rooms-board rules --as sol.a3a51677              # the standing part of the briefing again: who you are, the rules, where everyone works, browsers, the Consigliere's crew, room state
  bin/rooms-board state --as sol.a3a51677              # the room state
  bin/rooms-board keepalive --as sol.a3a51677          # the room's keep-alive setting
  bin/rooms-board crew --as sol.a3a51677               # your saved crew: name · model · effort level · worktree note · good at: … · avoid: …
  bin/rooms-board help

  # the Consigliere's (in a room without one, seat, state and keepalive are any member's):
  bin/rooms-board post "@opus write the plan" --as fable.a3a51677   # a request to that member; its reply wakes him
  bin/rooms-board correct 52 "…" --as fable.a3a51677   # amend his open request #52; cancel 52 withdraws it (or Backroom's request to a member: its seat briefing, its reseat's checkpoint)
  bin/rooms-board retry 57 --as fable.a3a51677         # run a failed turn again (#57 is its error notice or its request)
  bin/rooms-board state "…" --as fable.a3a51677        # replace the room state
  bin/rooms-board seat astra --as fable.a3a51677       # seats that saved crew member
  bin/rooms-board reseat opus "item 4" --as fable.a3a51677  # a checkpoint from @opus, then a fresh seat takes over with it
  bin/rooms-board retire opus --as fable.a3a51677      # @opus leaves the room (refused while it has work open; a briefing it never answered goes with it); its thread goes as your setting says, kept by default
  ```

  The `#51` a member is told to read is the `#51` you see. This also gives every member a way to read posts a long briefing shortened.

- **A context meter, and a reseat.** For a Claude thread, `members` and the member card say how much context its last call sent, and how many times Claude Code compacted it, from the thread's own transcript; past three quarters of the model's window it adds "near compaction". Other harnesses say "no meter". Nothing acts on it. When long work should go on from a clean context, the Consigliere runs `reseat <alias>`: the member is asked for a checkpoint (decisions and why, dead ends, the state of the code, the next step), which it writes when the work in hand is done; once it has answered and its thread is idle, a fresh seat of the same saved crew member is made in the same folder, the old seat's open requests and unread posts move to it in one handover with the checkpoint, the Consigliere's mark moves with it when it was his, and the old seat leaves the room (its T3 thread kept, settled, archived or deleted as **When he retires a member** says; kept by default). The checkpoint is Backroom's request, not yours: its answer wakes nobody, a checkpoint the member's thread fails is the Consigliere's notice (never on your **Needs you**), and he can `rooms-board cancel` it. If the checkpoint is cancelled, the member leaves the room, or the fresh seat cannot be made (T3 can no longer say where the member worked, say), the reseat is called off; the member keeps its seat and its work. The Consigliere is told on the board: with Backroom's request, which wakes him, when he has to act (the fresh seat could not be made; his answer goes to nobody), with a note otherwise.

Known limits: a member's reply that a correction overtook (it came in as the turn ended, too late to go in) wakes nobody; its next turn, which T3 runs at once, answers. A message to a member in a turn the room did not start (one typed in T3, or its own after its background work ended) waits for that turn to end. A message T3 gave up on and recorded only minutes later, after another turn had carried what it asked, is worked on twice. Background work T3 lists only after a member's answer is not named in that answer's status line, so only the member's own later turn reports it. A member can still message another with T3's own thread tools: it is recorded as a note and makes no request. A member's standing rules are in its seat briefing, not repeated with each request; `rooms-board rules` prints them, and they go out again after a compaction.
